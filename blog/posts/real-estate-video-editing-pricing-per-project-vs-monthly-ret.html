Real Estate Video Editing Pricing: Per Project vs Monthly Retainer for Videographers

Introduction

As a real estate videographer, you face an important business decision: should you charge clients per project or offer a monthly retainer for your video editing services? The answer affects your cash flow, workload, and client relationships. When you outsource your video post-production to a trusted video editing partner, you gain flexibility to offer both pricing models without overburdening your team. Let's break down the pros and cons of each approach.

Per Project Pricing

Per project pricing is straightforward: you quote a fixed fee for each property tour or video. This model works well for videographers with variable workloads or those just starting out.

Pros

  • Clear scope: Each project has a defined deliverable, making it easy to estimate editing time.
  • No long-term commitment: Clients pay for what they need, when they need it.
  • Simple invoicing: You bill per completed video, which aligns with client expectations.

Cons

  • Unpredictable income: Workload fluctuates, making financial planning harder.
  • Higher admin overhead: You spend time quoting each project separately.
  • Client hesitation: Some agents may hesitate to pay per project if they have frequent listings.

Monthly Retainer Pricing

A monthly retainer means the client pays a set fee each month for a predetermined number of edited videos. This model suits videographers with steady clients or those working with real estate teams.

Pros

  • Predictable revenue: You know your monthly income, which helps with budgeting and scaling.
  • Stronger client relationships: Retainers encourage long-term partnerships and consistent quality.
  • Efficiency: You can streamline your editing workflow with a production team, reducing turnaround times.

Cons

  • Scope creep risk: Clients may expect more than the agreed number of videos without extra pay.
  • Cash flow dependency: If a retainer client leaves, you lose a chunk of income.
  • Upfront negotiation: Setting fair terms requires careful planning and clear contracts.

Deciding What's Best for Your Business

Consider your current client base and growth goals. If you shoot occasional property tours, per project may be simpler. If you have consistent volume from real estate teams, a monthly retainer can stabilize your income. Many videographers use a hybrid approach: per project for new or occasional clients, retainers for loyal ones.

For retainer clients, you can guarantee consistent output because you're not limited by your own editing capacity. Frequently Asked Questions

Should I charge per project or monthly retainer for real estate video editing?

It depends on your workload consistency. Per project works for sporadic jobs, while a monthly retainer suits steady clients and provides predictable income for your video post-production.

How can outsourcing real estate video editing help with retainer pricing?

An outsourced video editing partner handles the post-production volume, so you can confidently offer retainers without worrying about editing capacity. This keeps your turnaround fast and quality high.

What should I include in a per project editing quote?

Include the number of property tour videos, length, revisions, and any extras like color grading or aerial footage. Clear scope prevents misunderstandings and protects your pricing.

Can I switch between per project and retainer pricing as a real estate videographer?

Yes, many videographers use a hybrid model. Start with per project for new clients, then move them to a monthly retainer once you establish trust and consistent volume.
