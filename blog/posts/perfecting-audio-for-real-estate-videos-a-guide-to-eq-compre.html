Perfecting Audio for Real Estate Videos: A Guide to EQ, Compression, and Music Ducking

Introduction to Audio Mixing for Real Estate Videos

As a real estate videographer or production team, you understand the importance of high-quality audio in your property tour videos. Good audio can make or break the viewer's experience, and it's essential to get it right. In this article, we'll explore the key techniques for audio mixing, including EQ, compression, and background music ducking, to help you create professional-sounding real estate videos.

Understanding EQ and Its Role in Audio Mixing

EQ, or equalization, is the process of adjusting the tone of your audio to ensure it sounds balanced and clear. In the context of real estate video editing, EQ can be used to reduce unwanted frequencies, such as low rumbles or high-pitched squeaks, and enhance the clarity of the agent's voice.

Applying EQ to Agent Walkthroughs

When applying EQ to your agent walkthroughs, start by identifying the frequency range of the agent's voice. Typically, this falls between 100 Hz and 8 kHz. Use a parametric EQ to boost or cut specific frequencies, and be careful not to overdo it, as this can lead to an unnatural sound.

Compression: Controlling Dynamic Range

Compression is another essential technique in audio mixing, used to control the dynamic range of your audio. This means reducing the difference between the loudest and quietest parts of the recording, resulting in a more even and polished sound.

Using Compression in Real Estate Video Editing

In real estate video editing, compression can be used to even out the agent's voice, ensuring that loud and quiet parts are balanced. Be cautious not to over-compress, as this can lead to a lifeless sound. A good starting point is to use a ratio of 3:1 to 5:1, with a threshold set to around -20 dB.

Background Music Ducking: Creating a Balanced Sound

Background music ducking is a technique used to automatically reduce the volume of background music when the agent is speaking. This ensures that the music doesn't overpower the voiceover and creates a balanced sound.

Implementing Music Ducking in Your Real Estate Videos

To implement music ducking, use a music ducking plugin or a digital audio workstation (DAW) with this feature. Set the threshold to around -20 dB, and adjust the ratio to taste. You can also use a sidechain input to trigger the ducking effect when the agent's voice is detected.
