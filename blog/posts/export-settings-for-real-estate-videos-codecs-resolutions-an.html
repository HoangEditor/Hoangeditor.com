Export Settings for Real Estate Videos: Codecs, Resolutions, and Delivery Specs

HE
Hoang Editor Team
October 5, 2026
4 min read
Share

As a real estate videographer, you've spent hours crafting the perfect property tour. But if your export settings are wrong, your footage can look soft, blocky, or suffer from color banding. Mastering export settings—codecs, resolutions, and delivery specs—is essential for delivering a polished final product that wows clients. Whether you handle video post-production in-house or rely on an outsourced video editing partner, getting these technical details right ensures your work stands out.

Understanding Codecs for Real Estate Video

A codec (compressor/decompressor) determines how your video file is encoded and decoded. The codec you choose affects file size, compatibility, and visual quality. For real estate video editing, the most common codecs are H.264 and H.265 (HEVC).

  • H.264: The industry standard for delivery. It balances quality and file size, making it ideal for online platforms and client previews.
  • H.265/HEVC: Offers roughly 50% better compression than H.264 at the same quality, but may have compatibility issues on older devices.
  • ProRes or DNxHD: Use these intermediate codecs for mastering high-quality versions before compressing to a delivery codec. Your video editing partner may request these if revisions are needed.

“Choosing the wrong codec can undo hours of color grading—always export a high-quality master first, then create platform-specific versions.”

Choosing the Right Resolution and Frame Rate

Resolution is the number of pixels in each frame, and frame rate is how many frames are displayed per second. For property tours, 4K (3840x2160) has become the standard for high-end listings, while 1080p (1920x1080) remains sufficient for faster loading on some MLS platforms.

Most real estate videos look best at 24 or 30 frames per second (fps) for a cinematic feel. If you're shooting with a drone or doing a fast walkthrough, 60fps can add smoothness, but it will increase file size. As a production team, we recommend shooting at 4K and delivering in 1080p if bandwidth is a concern.

Matching Frame Rate to Footage

Always export at the same frame rate as your source footage to avoid motion artifacts. If you shot at 60fps and want a cinematic look, you can interpret the footage to 24fps in post-production, but changing frame rates during export without proper conversion will cause stutter.

Bitrate and Compression: Balancing Quality vs File Size

Bitrate is the amount of data processed per second of video, measured in megabits per second (Mbps). Higher bitrates mean better quality but larger files. For 4K H.264 delivery, a bitrate of 35–45 Mbps is typical; for 1080p, 10–16 Mbps works well.

If you're delivering to a client on a USB drive, you can afford higher bitrates. For online streaming, lower bitrates help avoid buffering. An outsourced video editing service can provide optimized exports for each use case.

Delivery Specs for Different Platforms

Not all platforms play nice with every file type. Knowing where your video will end up helps you choose the right export settings.

  • YouTube & Vimeo: Use H.264, MP4 container, 4K at 30fps, bitrate 35–45 Mbps. YouTube processes everything, so a high-quality upload is key.
  • MLS & Real Estate Portals: Often limit file size and require 1080p H.264 MP4 at 8–12 Mbps. Check the specific MLS guidelines.
  • Social Media (Instagram, Facebook): 1080p, 30fps, H.264, 8 Mbps works. Vertical 4:5 or 9:16 variants may be needed.
  • Client Hard Drive or USB: For archiving or local playback, deliver a 4K ProRes master or a high-bitrate H.264 file.

Workflow Tips with Your Video Editing Partner

If you're working with an outsourced video editing team, clear communication about export specs will save time. Here's how to streamline the process:

  • Confirm the final delivery platform before the edit begins.
  • Provide your source footage frame rate and resolution.
  • Ask for both a master file and platform-specific versions if needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best codec for real estate video delivery?

H.264 is the safest choice for most real estate video delivery because it balances quality and compatibility. For 4K footage, H.265 can reduce file size but may not play on all devices.

Should I export in 4K or 1080p for MLS listings?

Most MLS platforms accept 1080p H.264 MP4 files at around 8–12 Mbps. 4K is great for YouTube or high-end clients, but 1080p is often more practical for property tours with upload limits.

What frame rate should I use for property tour videos?

24 or 30 frames per second gives a cinematic look for property tours. If you shot slow motion or fast walkthroughs, 60fps can be used, but always match your export frame rate to the source footage.

What bitrate should I use to avoid quality loss in real estate videos?

For 4K H.264 delivery, use 35–45 Mbps; for 1080p, 10–16 Mbps. Higher bitrates are fine for local playback, but lower bitrates help for online streaming without visible compression artifacts.
