Best Music Choices for Different Types of Real Estate Videos

HE
Hoang Editor Team
July 30, 2026
4 min read
Share

Music sets the emotional tone for any real estate video. A well-chosen soundtrack can turn a simple walkthrough into a compelling story, while a mismatched track can distract and disengage buyers. For real estate videographers and production teams, understanding the nuances of music selection is just as critical as the camera work and lighting. Whether you handle everything in-house or rely on outsourced video editing, choosing the right music will elevate your final deliverables.

Why Music Matters in Video Post-Production

During video post-production, the soundtrack isn’t background noise—it’s an active storytelling tool. The tempo, instrumentation, and mood guide viewers’ emotions and pace their viewing experience. A fast, upbeat track works for modern condos, while a slow, acoustic piece suits a cozy cottage. When working with a video editing partner, communicating these tonal preferences is essential for a cohesive result.

“Music is the shorthand of emotion. In real estate, it helps buyers imagine themselves living in the space. The right track makes a property feel like ‘home’ in seconds.” — Industry Pro

Music Choices by Property Type

Luxury Properties & High-End Estates

Luxury listings demand sophistication. Think sweeping orchestral scores, piano solos, or subtle cinematic strings. These tracks convey elegance, exclusivity, and timelessness. Avoid overly busy or upbeat music—simplicity lets the architecture and finishes take center stage. A slow, dramatic build works well for mansion tours and estate highlights.

  • Recommended instruments: Piano, cello, strings, soft brass
  • Tempo: Slow to moderate (60–90 BPM)
  • Examples: Ambient cinematic, neo-classical, soft jazz

Family Homes & Suburban Properties

Warmth and accessibility are key here. Acoustic guitar, light ukulele, or gentle indie folk create a welcoming atmosphere. These genres support a narrative of comfort, family, and everyday happiness. Upbeat acoustic tracks work well for quick property tours, while softer melodies suit longer walkthroughs.

  • Recommended instruments: Acoustic guitar, piano, light percussion, harmonica
  • Tempo: Moderate (80–110 BPM)
  • Examples: Indie folk, pop acoustic, light pop

Commercial Real Estate & Office Spaces

For commercial properties, professionalism and energy are essential. Corporate-style electronic, modern minimalist, or tech-forward beats signal competence and innovation. The music should feel dynamic but not distracting—it supports the visual of productivity and success.

  • Recommended instruments: Synth pads, electronic drums, bass, clean electric guitar
  • Tempo: Moderate to upbeat (100–130 BPM)
  • Examples: Ambient electronic, corporate pop, modern cinematic

New Developments & Aerial Showcases

Large-scale developments and drone footage call for epic, cinematic soundscapes. Orchestral rises, choir pads, and pulsating rhythmic elements build excitement and scale. These tracks make viewers feel the vast potential of the property.

  • Recommended instruments: Full orchestra, synth orchestra, percussion, choir
  • Tempo: Varied (slow build to crescendo)
  • Examples: Epic cinematic, cinematic hybrid, trailer music

Vacation Rentals & Lifestyle Properties

These videos aim to sell a lifestyle—vacation, relaxation, adventure. Chill tropical, lo-fi beats, or soft bossa nova can evoke travel vibes. The music should feel effortless and joyful, aligning with the property’s leisure appeal.

  • Recommended instruments: Steel drums, guitar, light synth, hand percussion
  • Tempo: Slow to moderate (70–100 BPM)
  • Examples: Tropical house, lo-fi chill, acoustic pop

Tips for Working with a Video Editing Partner

If you use outsourced video editing services, music selection becomes a collaborative process. Provide your video editing partner with reference tracks, mood boards, or even a short playlist. A professional production team can also recommend royalty-free libraries and customization options. Clear communication about tempo shifts, fade points, and volume mixing ensures the final property tour sounds as good as it looks.

  • Share examples of music you love (or hate) for reference.
  • Specify key moments in the video where music should build or drop.
  • Ask for options—your partner can layer multiple tracks for A/B testing.
  • Discuss licensing: ensure all music is cleared for commercial use.

With the right soundtrack, even a standard walkthrough becomes memorable. Whether you edit in-house or rely on a trusted video editing partner, prioritizing music selection in video post-production will set your work apart in a crowded market.
