Introduction to Drone Shots in Real Estate Video Editing

As a real estate videographer, capturing stunning aerial footage is crucial for showcasing properties in the best possible light. Two popular drone shots used in real estate video editing are the orbit and reveal shots. In this article, we will explore the differences between these two shots and discuss when to use each for maximum architectural impact.

Understanding Orbit Shots

Definition and Application

An orbit shot involves flying the drone in a circular motion around a subject, typically a building or a property. This shot is useful for showcasing the exterior features of a property, such as its architecture, landscaping, and surroundings.

Best Practices for Orbit Shots

To capture a stunning orbit shot, it's essential to consider the position of the sun, the height of the drone, and the speed of the orbit. A well-executed orbit shot can add a touch of professionalism to your real estate video editing project.

Understanding Reveal Shots

Definition and Application

A reveal shot involves flying the drone over a subject, typically revealing a property or a feature in a dramatic and dynamic way. This shot is useful for creating a sense of excitement and wonder, drawing the viewer's attention to a particular aspect of the property.

Best Practices for Reveal Shots

To capture a stunning reveal shot, it's essential to consider the starting point of the shot, the trajectory of the drone, and the ending point of the shot. A well-executed reveal shot can add a touch of drama and flair to your real estate video editing project.

When to Use Each Shot

The choice between an orbit shot and a reveal shot depends on the specific goals of your real estate video editing project. If you want to showcase the exterior features of a property, an orbit shot may be the better choice. However, if you want to create a sense of drama and excitement, a reveal shot may be more effective.

Orbit Shot Use Cases

  • Showcasing the exterior features of a property
  • Highlighting the architecture and landscaping of a property
  • Creating a sense of context and surroundings

Reveal Shot Use Cases

  • Creating a sense of drama and excitement
  • Drawing attention to a particular feature of a property
  • Adding a touch of flair to a real estate video editing project

By understanding the strengths and weaknesses of each shot, you can make informed decisions about when to use each shot in your real estate video editing project. Whether you're working with a real estate videographer, a production team, or an outsourced video editing partner, the key is to choose the shot that best showcases the property and achieves your goals.
