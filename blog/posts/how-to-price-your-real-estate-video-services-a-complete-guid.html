How to Price Your Real Estate Video Services: A Complete Guide for Videographers

HE
Hoang Editor Team
July 23, 2026
4 min read

As a real estate videographer, one of the most challenging aspects of your business is determining how to price your services. Charge too little, and you'll struggle to stay profitable. Charge too much, and you may scare away potential clients. In this guide, we'll break down the key factors to consider when pricing your real estate video services, from property tours to drone footage, and how to maximize your profit margins.

Why Pricing Matters for Real Estate Videographers

Your pricing strategy directly impacts your income, client relationships, and the perceived value of your work. A well-thought-out pricing model helps you attract the right clients—those who understand the value of high-quality real estate video editing and are willing to pay for it.

“The goal isn’t to be the cheapest videographer in town. It’s to be the best value.”

Factors to Consider When Pricing Real Estate Video Services

1. Your Skill Level and Experience

Are you just starting out, or are you a seasoned pro with a portfolio of stunning property tours? Experienced videographers with a proven track record can command higher rates. If you’re new, consider offering competitive pricing to build your portfolio, but avoid undervaluing yourself.

2. The Scope of the Project

Not all real estate videos are created equal. A simple walkthrough tour will cost less than a cinematic property tour with drone footage, interviews, and advanced color grading. Break down your services into tiers:

  • Basic Package: Single property walkthrough, 1-2 minutes, minimal editing
  • Standard Package: Multiple rooms, drone shots, 2-3 minutes, music and basic color correction
  • Premium Package: Full cinematic tour, aerial footage, interviews, advanced real estate video editing, 3-5 minutes

3. Location and Market Demand

Rates vary by city and region. A videographer in New York City can charge more than one in a rural area. Research local competitors to see what the market will bear.

4. Post-Production Time

Real estate video editing takes time—sometimes more than the shoot itself. If you’re editing in-house, factor in your hourly rate for editing. Alternatively, you can use outsourced editing services like Hoang Editor to reduce your workload and increase your profit margins. By outsourcing editing, you can focus on shooting more projects and scale your business without sacrificing quality.

Popular Pricing Models for Real Estate Video Services

Per Project Pricing

This is the most common model. You charge a flat fee for each video project. For example, $500 for a standard property tour. This gives clients clarity and allows you to budget your time.

Hourly Pricing

Some videographers charge by the hour, especially for complex projects. Rates typically range from $75 to $200 per hour, depending on experience. However, clients prefer flat fees because they know the upfront cost.

Monthly Retainers

For real estate agents who list multiple properties per month, offer a monthly retainer. For example, $1,500 per month for up to 4 property tours. This provides steady income and builds long-term relationships.

How Outsourced Editing Boosts Your Profit Margin

One of the biggest expenses for videographers is post-production time. By outsourcing your real estate video editing to a professional service like Hoang Editor, you can:

  • Focus on shooting more properties

Tips for Communicating Your Pricing to Clients

  • Be transparent: Explain what each package includes and why the price is justified.
  • Showcase your value: Share examples of past property tours that led to faster sales or higher offers.
  • Offer add-ons: Upgrades like 4K resolution, same-day turnaround, or social media clips can increase revenue.

Final Thoughts

Pricing your real estate video services doesn’t have to be a guessing game. By understanding your costs, researching the market, and leveraging outsourced editing, you can set rates that are competitive and profitable. Remember, your time is valuable—don’t give it away for free.
