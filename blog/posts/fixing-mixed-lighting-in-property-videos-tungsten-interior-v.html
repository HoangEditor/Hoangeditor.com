Fixing Mixed Lighting in Property Videos: Tungsten Interior vs Daylight Windows

Introduction to Mixed Lighting

Mixed lighting can be a challenge for real estate videographers when capturing property tours. The combination of tungsten interior lighting and daylight pouring in through windows can create unflattering color casts and make it difficult to achieve a consistent look.

Understanding the Problem

The human eye can adjust to different lighting conditions, but cameras can struggle to capture a wide range of colors and brightness levels. This can result in footage that looks unnatural or unappealing.

Tungsten Interior Lighting

Tungsten lighting, commonly used in indoor fixtures, produces a warm, yellowish glow. This type of lighting can create a cozy atmosphere, but it can also clash with the cool, bluish tones of daylight.

Daylight Windows

Daylight pouring in through windows can add a nice touch of natural light to a room, but it can also create harsh shadows and highlights. When combined with tungsten interior lighting, it can be difficult to balance the two and achieve a consistent color temperature.

Solutions for Fixing Mixed Lighting

Fortunately, there are several solutions for fixing mixed lighting in property videos. One approach is to use color grading techniques to balance the color temperature of the footage. This can be done using video editing software, such as Adobe Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve.

Another approach is to use lighting fixtures that can match the color temperature of the daylight. For example, using LED lights with a high color rendering index (CRI) can help to create a more natural and consistent look.

Outsourced Video Editing

A professional editor can use their expertise and software to balance the color temperature and create a consistent look throughout the footage.
