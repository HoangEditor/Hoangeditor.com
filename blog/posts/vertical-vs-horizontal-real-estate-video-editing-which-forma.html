The Format Dilemma: Vertical vs Horizontal

Real estate video editing traditionally followed broadcast and cinematic standards: horizontal 16:9. But the explosion of mobile-first platforms like TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts has forced real estate videographers to rethink their deliverables. A single property tour now often needs multiple cuts in different aspect ratios.

Horizontal (16:9) – The Classic Choice

Horizontal video remains the standard for high-production property tours. It offers a wider field of view, allowing you to capture more of the room, architectural details, and natural light. It’s ideal for websites, YouTube, and presentations where viewers watch on larger screens.

  • Pros: Immersive wide-angle shots that showcase entire rooms; familiar cinematic feel that builds trust with high-end clients; easier to frame during shooting – no need to worry about vertical crop.
  • Cons: Less optimized for mobile feeds, where most social media consumption happens; may require viewers to rotate their phones or watch with black bars; lower engagement rates on TikTok and Instagram Reels.

Vertical (9:16) – The Mobile Powerhouse

Vertical video has taken over social media. Platforms like Instagram Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts are designed for full-screen vertical viewing. For a property tour, vertical video feels more intimate and immediate – as if the viewer is walking through the home on their phone.

  • Pros: Maximizes screen real estate on smartphones; higher engagement rates on mobile-first platforms; native to Stories and Reels, where real estate agents are most active.
  • Cons: Crops out peripheral details – you may miss wide rooms or exterior shots; requires careful framing during shooting or reframing in post-production; less suitable for traditional websites or TV displays.

Which Format Wins for Social Media?

The short answer: vertical wins for social media. According to a study by HubSpot, vertical videos have a 90% higher completion rate compared to horizontal videos on mobile. For real estate marketing, that means more potential buyers actually watch the entire property tour instead of scrolling past.

“We used to deliver everything in 16:9, but once we started posting vertical cuts on Instagram Reels, our client’s engagement tripled. Now every shoot includes both formats.” – Sarah M., Real Estate Videographer

However, that doesn’t mean horizontal is dead. Many real estate agents still want a polished horizontal version for their website, YouTube channel, or email campaigns. The winning strategy is a hybrid approach: shoot in high resolution horizontal (4K or higher) and deliver both formats, or use a video editing partner to create vertical crops from the same footage.

How to Master Both Formats Without Losing Your Mind

Creating two versions of every property tour doubles your editing workload. That’s where outsourced video editing comes in. A professional video post-production team can take your raw footage and deliver optimized cuts for every platform.

Tips for Shooting for Dual Formats

  • Shoot in 4K or higher resolution to allow for cropping without quality loss
  • Frame wide shots with headroom and side space to accommodate vertical crop
  • Use a gimbal or stabilizer for smooth movement in both orientations
  • Capture key features like kitchens and master baths in both wide and tight shots

The battle between vertical and horizontal isn’t about “either/or” – it’s about using the right format for the right platform. By shooting smart and leveraging outsourced video editing, you can deliver a complete package that maximizes impact on social media while still impressing on traditional channels.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I shoot vertical or horizontal for real estate videos?

It depends on the platform. Shoot horizontal in high resolution and then crop to vertical for social media, or shoot both if possible. An outsourced video editing team can handle the conversion.

Can I use the same footage for vertical and horizontal edits?

Yes, if you shoot in 4K or higher with extra space around the subject, you can crop to vertical without significant quality loss. A video post-production expert can reframe shots perfectly.

What is the best format for Instagram and TikTok real estate videos?

Vertical 9:16 is best for Instagram Reels and TikTok because it fills the phone screen and increases engagement. Real estate videographers should deliver vertical cuts for these platforms.

How can outsourced video editing help with format differences?

An outsourced video editing partner can create multiple versions of your property tour, handling cropping, reframing, and platform-specific edits so your production team saves time.
