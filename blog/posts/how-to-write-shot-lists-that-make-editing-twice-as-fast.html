How to Write Shot Lists That Make Editing Twice as Fast

HE
Hoang Editor Team
August 10, 2026
4 min read
Share

For a real estate videographer, the shoot is only half the battle. The real clock-wrecker is video post-production. If you're sending your footage to an outsourced video editing team, the quality of your shot list can make the difference between a lightning-fast turnaround and a frustrating week of back-and-forth.

A well-crafted shot list isn't just a to-do checklist for shooting. It's a communication tool that tells your editor the story before they ever open the footage. Here's how to write a shot list that makes the entire property tour editing process twice as fast.

Why Your Shot List Affects Post-Production Speed

Your editor has to make hundreds of decisions: which take to use, where to place a shot, how long to hold it. Without a clear map, they spend hours scrubbing through raw files and guessing your intent. A good shot list eliminates that guesswork.

A poorly organized shot list doesn't just slow down your editor—it raises your bill. Clarity in the field equals speed in the edit bay.

The 5 Elements Every Shot List Needs

1. Standardized Naming Conventions

Use a consistent format that includes the property address, room, and take number. For example: 123Main_Kitchen_Broll_02. This lets your editor sort clips instantly and identify missing shots without contacting you.

2. Segment Blocking

Group shots by the final edit structure: Exterior, Entry, Living Room, Kitchen, Bedrooms, Outdoor. This mirrors the flow of the finished property tour and lets your editor assemble sequences in order, rather than jumping all over the timeline.

3. Must-Have vs. Optional Shots

Mark your hero shots with a color or a star. These are the non-negotiables. Use a “maybe” tag for bonus angles. The editor will prioritize the heroes and only add extras if time allows—so you never lose a critical shot to a creative tangent.

4. Desired Camera Movement

Specify whether each shot is a gimbal glide, slider, drone flyover, or static tripod. This helps your editor plan transitions and match motion between clips—avoiding jarring match cuts that require extra correction.

5. Timing and Duration Notes

Indicate how long to hold each shot or what the intended on-screen duration is. A simple note like “hold for verse” or “use for intro, 10s” can save your editor from guessing at pacing.

How to Structure the List for Faster Assembly

Think of your shot list as an edit script. Start from the front door and work through the house in the order a buyer would walk. Include an establishing drone shot, a few close-ups of fixtures, and transition shots like door handles or windows.

Use a table with four columns: Shot number, Description, Movement, and Purpose. Keep one row per shot. This makes it easy for your editor to cross-reference with the footage and build the timeline in the same order.

Moving From Shot List to Edit Deck (For Outsourced Work)

When you work with a professional real estate video editing partner, your shot list becomes the foundation of the edit deck. Add a column for “editor notes” where you can write things like “Use this as the background for the agent intro” or “Color-grade cool to match the exterior.”

Your video editing partner doesn't know the property like you do—they only know what you record and share. A detailed shot list transfers your vision directly, so you don't have to micromanage later. For our production team at Hoang Editor, clients who provide structured shot lists often see their edits delivered in a third of the time compared to those who send disorganized folders.

Common Shot List Mistakes That Waste Time

  • Vague descriptions like “kitchen shot” – be specific with angle and subject.
  • Overloading with duplicates – avoid shooting 10 versions of the same door without marking the best.
  • No sequence order – make the list match the story flow, not your shooting convenience.
  • Ignoring audio notes – if you want ambient sounds or music cues, write those down.

Take 15 minutes before each shoot to plan your shot list properly. That tiny investment will save hours in video post-production and make you the favorite client of any outsourced video editing team.
